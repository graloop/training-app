The tour

Everything about your training, in five tabs

The examples below are the real app with made-up data.

Calendar

Planned and done, side by side

Each session you planned sits on its day. Once Garmin records it, it turns green: solid when you did it as planned, dashed when the duration was different. Sleep score and resting heart rate are on every day, and each week adds up its hours and load.

  • Syncs with Garmin on its own, a few times a day
  • Plan, edit or move a session in two clicks
  • Weekly hours, training load, sleep and resting heart rate
Plan

Claude writes the plan. You decide.

Ask Claude in Chrome to read your history and write the coming weeks, one line per session. The preview shows each week and its split by sport. Nothing reaches your calendar until you press the button.

  • One to twelve weeks at a time
  • Your rules are followed: schedule, exams, rest days
  • Edit any line yourself before it goes on the calendar
Athlete

Your races and your numbers

Your objectives with a countdown, your tested numbers and zones, and what Garmin knows about you. Everything here is read when a plan is written, so it is built for your next race.

  • Race date, target time and what matters most
  • FTP, threshold pace, CSS and heart rate zones
  • Your own numbers win over Garmin's estimates

Draft plan

        
The text the preview above is made from. Claude in Chrome writes it here, and you can edit it yourself.


        
        
        
Format: one session per line, written as date | sport | duration | details. Date like Tue Oct 13 or 2026-10-13. Sport: swim, bike, run or strength. Duration like 1:15 or 45 min. Details are optional (the workout itself). Two sessions on one day are two lines. Lines starting with # are comments, and rest days need no line.


        

          
          
        


        

      


      

        
Rule set

        
The rules every plan follows: when you can train, school and work schedule, exam periods, holidays and travel, the weekly rhythm, rules of thumb (rest when resting HR is up, long run on Sunday…).
